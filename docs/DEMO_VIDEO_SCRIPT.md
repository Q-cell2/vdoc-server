# VDoc — A Document That Listens

Three-minute hackathon product video script | English narration | Target length: 2:55

**Creative direction:** Clean, Gemini-style tech aesthetic combined with real product screen recordings. One document carries the whole story: voice creation, precise editing, Fast / Deep modes, and the role of Boson Higgs.

## 0:00–0:20 | Opening: the problem

**Visual**
A black background slowly reveals a blank document. Quick cuts: typing, deleting, adjusting formatting, copying and pasting, revising again and again. Keyboard clicks are audible as the background music fades in.

**Narration**
> "Every great document starts with an idea.
> But getting from an idea to a finished document takes so much work.
> Writing, editing, formatting, revising…
> What if all of that could be simpler?"

**On-screen text**
WHAT IF YOUR DOCUMENT COULD LISTEN?

## 0:20–0:40 | Introducing VDoc

**Visual**
The music builds. The full VDoc interface fades in. The camera glides from the editor down to the microphone button at the bottom, and the VDoc logo appears at the center of the screen.

**Narration**
> "Introducing VDoc.
> A voice-first AI document editor, powered by Boson Higgs.
> No complex prompts. No endless copying and pasting.
> Just say what you have in mind.
> VDoc takes care of the rest."

**On-screen text**
vdoc. YOUR VOICE. YOUR DOCUMENT.

## 0:40–1:10 | Feature 1: Create a document by voice

**Visual**
Open a completely blank document. Tap the microphone and begin a real voice interaction. Show Boson processing the voice request. The document starts writing itself: the title, paragraphs, and event schedule appear in the editor one after another.

**Narration**
> "With Boson Higgs, VDoc understands your spoken instructions and turns your ideas into a real, editable document.
> From a simple idea to a complete first draft.
> Without ever touching the keyboard."

**Live command**
> "Write an event plan for a University of Toronto AI Workshop, including an introduction, three main goals, and the event schedule."

**On-screen text**
SPEAK → CREATE

## 1:10–1:40 | Feature 2: Edit a document by voice

**Visual**
The freshly generated plan is on screen, and the camera zooms in on the introduction. Show the AI making targeted edits: the introduction gets shorter and the three goals become a bulleted list, while everything else stays untouched.

**Narration**
> "But VDoc is more than a document generator.
> It understands what you want to change.
> Tell it what needs fixing, the way you'd tell a friend.
> No need to regenerate the whole thing.
> Your ideas always stay in one place."

**Live command**
> "Make the introduction a bit shorter, and turn the three goals into a bulleted list."

**On-screen text**
DON'T JUST GENERATE. EDIT.

## 1:40–2:15 | Feature 3: Fast and Deep thinking modes

**Visual**
The camera moves to the thinking-mode selector. Fast mode lights up, then switches to Deep mode. If available, capture the real processing state of Boson calling Claude. Then show the newly inserted budget, risk analysis, and execution plan.

**Narration**

> Simple edits should be quick. Complex writing needs deeper analysis.
> That's why we built two thinking modes.
> In Fast mode, VDoc uses Boson's built-in capabilities to understand and finish the task quickly.
> In Deep mode, Boson can call on Claude to handle more complex reasoning and writing."

**Live command**
> "Use Deep mode to add a budget analysis, three potential risks, and a detailed execution plan for this event."

**On-screen text**
FAST WHEN YOU NEED SPEED. DEEP WHEN YOU NEED MORE.

## Production and editing notes

- Core sections must use real feature recordings. Duck the music during voice commands and keep the original audio and actual results.
- If targeted editing, the Claude status, or export isn't working yet, adjust the visuals and narration to match what actually runs.
- Judging weights: Higgs 30% / UX 25% / Innovation 25% / Technical 20%. Video must be 2–3 minutes; this script plans for about 2:55.
- English narration runs longer than Chinese at the same pace. Time each section after recording the voiceover and trim lines if a section overruns.
